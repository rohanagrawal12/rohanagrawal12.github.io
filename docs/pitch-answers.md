# Bulletin: answers you can paste into any box
Use what fits the box. Items in [brackets] are things only you can fill in (real numbers).

Business name: Bulletin
Tagline: Find your thing.
One-sentence description: Bulletin is a free website where students take a two-minute quiz and get every club, volunteer spot and program ranked by how well it fits them.

Hook (slide 1): "A school can have 98 clubs and teams, and most students could name five." [use a real number if you survey]
Problem: Clubs are scattered across spreadsheets, flyers and group chats. Volunteering and programs are somewhere else. Students miss things they would love because they never heard about them. New clubs (like ones started by ninth graders) have no easy way to be found.
How you know it is a problem: I gave a prototype to 8 classmates and collected written feedback. [Add: "X of Y students said they have missed a club they would have joined." Run the 5-question survey below.]
Solution: A quiz in the student's own words, plus a match level from 1 to 10 for every opportunity, with School, Community and Explore in one list. Each listing shows when it meets, who it suits, how to join and key dates.
Value to the customer: Convenience (one place), Quality (clear times, rooms, deadlines), USP (open-text quiz matching, all three sources together), Branding (friendly, simple, student-made), Design (clean, works on phone and computer).
Target audience:
- Demographic: students about 13-18 (middle school, high school; college later), parents and school staff as secondary users.
- Geographic: Houston, Texas, starting with The Village School.
- Psychographic: curious, involved or wanting to be, building college applications, want service hours.
- Behavioral: on their phones daily, search for clubs by interest, ask friends for recommendations.
Competitors: RoundPier, ClubConnect, ClubHub, C!ub, Laker Launchpad and MDex. Most are tied to one college or one school's calendar. Bulletin matches by what you write and covers school, community and online options.
Evidence of demand: 8 classmates gave feedback; most liked the phone design and the quiz; the top complaint was quiz length, so I shortened it. [Add survey results and number of quiz completions.]
What you did to develop it (process): Google Form quiz, then feedback from 8 classmates, then a redesign (shorter quiz, simpler list, dark mode, new name and logo), then a working website with a matching engine, then a database plan with Supabase. Roadblocks: quiz too long (shortened); the first name, Launchpad, was already used by other student programs (rebranded to Bulletin); the first design felt too busy (simplified).
Customer outreach: shared prototype and form with classmates; next, student government, the activities director and club sponsors.
Sales and distribution plan: Free for students. They open a link (or QR code on a poster) and can add it to a phone home screen. Schools join through counselors and the activities office. Later revenue: schools pay a yearly fee for a club dashboard (approvals, sign-ups, reports). Students never pay.
Revenue model (pick one): school licenses first; sponsored events from organizations later (with school approval).
Next steps: connect real accounts (Supabase), club leader tools with admin approval, notifications for deadlines, more schools in Houston, keep opportunity listings current.
The Ask: [Choose a real number.] Example: "$1,500 for a domain, hosting upgrade, a school pilot and posters. $500 for a part-time student ambassador at each school."
Key metrics to track: quiz completions, saves, Explore taps, return visits.

## 5-question survey for evidence (send as a Google Form)
1. How do you find out about clubs at school? (friends, flyers, teachers, website, I don't)
2. Have you ever missed a club or activity you would have joined? (yes/no)
3. How easy is it to see all clubs in one place? (1-5)
4. Would you use an app that ranks clubs by what you like? (1-5)
5. What would make you use it every week? (short answer)
